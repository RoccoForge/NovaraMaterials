The tuning dials

How we tailor a heater to your application.

Your application sets two inputs. We turn four independent dials until the heater meets them, then verify the design against four constraints.

Inputs we design to

Two things about your product decide everything else.

Thermal load

The thing being heated, and the thermal job to be done.

  • Target material: mass, heat capacity, conductivity
  • Required temperature, ramp rate and hold tolerance
  • Contact quality at the heater-target interface
  • Duty profile: continuous, cyclic or on-demand
What it sets

The energy the heater must deliver, and where — the spec every dial is turned against.

Environment

The conditions the heater lives in — and loses heat to.

  • Ambient temperature range and extremes
  • Airflow and convective losses
  • Insulation, mounting and surrounding structure
  • Moisture, altitude, vibration, chemical exposure
What it sets

The loss budget and derating — the same heater behaves differently in still air and a 60-mph slipstream.

Dials we turn

Four design vectors, independently adjustable.

Two structural directions — same content.

01

Geometry

The layout of the bus bars and the size and shape of the heater.

  • Bus bar placement, spacing and cross-section
  • Heater footprint, aspect ratio and cutouts
  • Zoned or patterned coatings for differential watt density
  • Lead exit and termination locations
What it sets

Watt-density distribution, the resistance window at a given sheet resistance, and thermal uniformity across the part.

02

Chemistry

The formulation — the film we lay down and how it bonds to the stack.

  • CNT loading in the dispersion (sheet resistance)
  • Binders and matrix components
  • Adhesives and substrate interface
  • Coating thickness and layer count
What it sets

Base resistance, maximum operating temperature, flexibility, adhesion and long-term stability.

03

Electricity

How power reaches the film.

  • AC vs. DC drive
  • Supply voltage and available current
  • PWM duty cycle and switching strategy
  • Drive electronics and connector scheme
What it sets

Heat-up rate, steady-state power, efficiency, and compatibility with the host system’s power budget.

04

Sensing & control

How the system knows the temperature — and holds it.

  • Thermistors, RTDs or thermocouples in the stack
  • Open-loop vs. closed-loop control logic
  • Film TCR and self-regulating behavior
  • Over-temperature cutoffs and fail-safes
What it sets

Accuracy, repeatability, response to disturbances, and the safety case.

Couplings

Turn one dial and others move. Here's how we manage it.

Chemistry → geometry + electricity

Change CNT loading and sheet resistance moves — the bus bar math and required drive voltage move with it.

Geometry → electricity

A larger heated area at the same sheet resistance lowers total resistance and raises current draw on the supply.

Environment → geometry + chemistry

Higher convective losses demand more watt density, which requires a more aggressive thermal response.

Thermal load → sensing

Tight hold tolerances can't be met open-loop — the spec itself decides whether feedback is required.

The alternatives

Legacy heaters give you one dial — maybe two.

Conventional technologies fix the material up front, leaving geometry as almost the only thing you can change. Uniformity, maximum temperature, flexibility and resistance then move together — one lever drags the rest.

Nichrome wire

A resistance wire routed across a surface.

  • Heat concentrates along the wire — hot lines, cold gaps between runs
  • Bulky and rigid; fatigues and breaks under bending or vibration
  • One break opens the entire circuit
Dials you get: geometry only

Etched copper foil

A copper trace photo-etched onto polyimide film.

  • High temperature coefficient — resistance climbs with heat, inviting hot-spot runaway
  • Heat follows the serpentine trace; gaps between runs stay cold
  • Resistance boxed in by fixed foil thickness; traces crack under flex
Dials you get: geometry + a fixed metal

Silver paste

Conductive silver ink screen-printed into a pattern.

  • Silver migrates under bias and humidity — dendrites, shorts, drift
  • Narrow sheet-resistance window; high resistance is hard to reach
  • Cost scales with silver-heavy coverage; prints crack under flex
Dials you get: print pattern + thickness

Novara CNT film

The film itself is the heater.

  • Uniform heat across the whole surface — no traces to trace
  • Flexible and damage-tolerant; a local defect doesn't open the circuit
  • Sheet resistance is formulated, not inherited from a metal
Dials you get: all four, independently
Constraints

Every design is checked against four constraints.

Integration & packaging

Stack-up, encapsulation, bend radius, attachment to the host assembly.

Manufacturability

Coating process window, sheet-resistance uniformity, yield at volume.

Reliability & lifetime

Thermal cycling, aging, environmental durability over the service life.

Safety & regulatory

Touch-temperature limits, certifications, failure-mode behavior.
