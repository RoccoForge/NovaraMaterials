Why carbon nanotube

You shouldn't have to choose between heating performance and product design.

Everyone else heats wires - we heat surfaces. The film itself is the heater — so warmth spreads evenly across the whole surface, in a layer thin and flexible enough to disappear into your product.

01

Ultra-thin and lightweight

Less than .010 inches thick at about 1.2 oz/ft². It adds almost no weight and takes almost no space in your assembly.

02

Uniform heat, no hot spots

Heat disperses across the entire film rather than along a trace, giving a flat temperature profile and a lower maximum operating temperature.

03

Fast to temperature

Our heating layer is uniformly dispersed right at the surface, or just benath, there's almost no mass to warm before you feel it — heat that's instant, not gradual.

04

Flexible in material and shape

Custom-cut or bent to fit nearly any geometry, and customizable to your voltage and power requirements.

05

Simple to integrate

Attach with adhesives or fasteners to almost any surface. Typically deposited on film, it can also be applied to other substrates.

The material

A lattice, not a wire.

Carbon nanotubes conduct and distribute heat orders of magnitude more efficiently than conventional resistive wire, at a fraction of the weight and thickness. The science originates from Battelle, refined across more than fifteen years of automotive and aerospace research.

Measured proof

Direct-contact battery heating, measured against a coolant loop.

A conventional high-voltage coolant heater warms fluid, pumps it, and transfers it through a cold plate before it reaches a cell. Our film heats the battery cells directly, through incorporation on the cooling ribbons, cold plates or sandwiched between cells.

One film, two jobs

Our film replaces the dielectric coating already on cooling ribbons and cold plates, turning a passive insulator into an active heater — no added packaging volume.

The HVCH comes out

Heating the cell directly removes the coolant heater, pump, manifolds and plumbing — fewer components, fewer failure modes, less mass in a space-constrained pack.

Warmth before charge

Bringing cells above 0°C before current flows avoids the lithium plating that drives cold-climate capacity loss and premature pack replacement.
